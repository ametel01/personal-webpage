## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored in content, conventional in composition.** The restrained editorial typography, evidence language, project marks, merged-PR counts, and emphasis on provenance and correctness clearly belong to Alex’s engineering position. It does not feel like a generic junior portfolio.

Most of that specificity lives in the copy, however. Structurally, the page is still a familiar portfolio sequence: manifesto hero, fact panel, equal-weight card grid, skills inventory, experience, and contact. In Experience mode, the work should be the first-viewport protagonist. ## Overall Impression

The page feels serious, truthful, and professionally engineered. Its biggest weakness is that it explains Alex before it demonstrates Alex. The strongest proof moments—public artifacts, merged contributions, release state, and shipped systems—are buried inside a long, repetitive inventory rather than composed into a memorable first impression.

- **Desktop hierarchy is controlled.** At 1440×900, the hero and credibility panel form a coherent opening, with selected work beginning to enter the viewport.
- **The responsive and semantic foundation is sound.** No horizontal overflow appeared at 390 px; headings, landmarks, labeled navigation, definition lists, safe external links, focus styles, and reduced-motion support are present.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The artifact does not lead, especially on mobile

**Why it matters:** Experience mode should put the work in front of evaluators immediately. At 390×844, the first project begins around y=1,333 px; the opening viewport contains positioning copy and CTAs rather than inspectable work.

**Fix:** Reduce the mobile headline scale, line count, and hero footprint. Promote AgentReceipt or another lead project into the opening composition with a truthful compact proof module: current state, ownership, one evidence artifact, and one case-study action. Keep credibility facts adjacent instead of forcing them below a manifesto-length opening.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] “Selected Work” gives all seven projects equal weight

**Why it matters:** Engineering managers scanning in 30–60 seconds need editorial judgment. Seven equal cards plus “View all projects” creates an eight-option decision and a visibly unresolved 4+3 desktop grid.

**Fix:** Keep all seven canonical projects on `/work`, but curate the homepage around one featured project and three supporting projects that collectively prove AI tooling, backend/platform depth, and blockchain infrastructure. Add a concise “View all 7 case studies” path. If all seven must remain, group them into no more than three capability lanes and establish one unmistakable primary card.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Several mobile actions miss the 44 px touch-target floor

**Why it matters:** At 390 px, GitHub and LinkedIn each measured only 16 px high despite spanning the row. Open-source arrow buttons measured 38×38 px, and project text links also require precise tapping.

**Fix:** Give `.home-social-link`, `.home-project-link`, `.section-text-link`, and `.open-source-link` at least `min-height: 44px`, with balanced horizontal padding and clear separation. Preserve the current 56 px header navigation targets.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Proof copy is dense and repeats the summaries

**Why it matters:** Project cards reach roughly 350 px high on desktop and 310 px on mobile. Long proof paragraphs, clipped summaries, four tags, and identical “View Project” links slow evaluation where evidence should accelerate it.

**Fix:** Rewrite each homepage proof as one verifiable clause: evidence type, current state, and differentiator. Move exhaustive capability lists to case studies. Use destination-specific labels such as “AgentReceipt case study” so links remain recognizable out of context.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Card behavior and the visual world remain category-interchangeable

**Why it matters:** Each project card lifts and changes border color on hover, implying the whole surface is clickable, but only “View Project” navigates. More broadly, cards and tags communicate competence without embodying the distinctive ideas of provenance, observability, or correctness-sensitive work.

**Fix:** Make the card’s primary content area one semantic link or remove whole-card hover affordance. Replace some generic card chrome with one reusable evidence-derived motif—trace rows, signed-artifact metadata, verifier-state flow, or system boundaries—using only facts already present in the case studies.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable bolder`
